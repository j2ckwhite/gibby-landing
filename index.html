Why it sits there

Nobody in the shop owns the quote after it's sent.

It isn't a demand problem and it isn't an ad problem. The work is coming in. It leaks out between the quote and the booking, and it leaks for the same four reasons in almost every shop we've looked at.

01 Follow-up

Follow-up stops at one.

Most shops send the price, maybe one "just checking in," then move on. Three touches converts far more than one. Almost nobody gets to three, because nobody's job is to.

02 The owner

You are the sales department.

You quote, you chase, you book, and you also run the shop. Chasing a $2,400 quote is a $2,400 task. It keeps losing to the $10 tasks that shout louder in the moment.

03 Slow season

Winter arrives and the list is already dead.

When the bays go quiet, the only leads you have are the ones you didn't close in summer. Most shops have never gone back to them, so the slow season stays slow.

04 No scoreboard

No one knows the number.

Ask most owners how many quotes went quiet last year and the honest answer is "no idea." You can't fix a leak you've never measured. We measure it first, for free.

The part most shops get wrong

An automated follow-up bot is not the fix. It's how you lose them for good.

The CRMs will sell you a generic AI text sequence. Your customers can smell it in one line, and the shop's name is on it. What works is a real person, writing from the actual quote, under your shop's name.

Generic bot follow-up
Hi! 👋 Just following up on your recent inquiry. We'd love to earn your business! Are you still interested in our services?
Hey there! We noticed you haven't booked yet. Don't miss out, book today!
— no reply —
Reads as spam. Mentions nothing about them. Trains the customer to ignore your number.
